  • [ DEVLOG ] Two Years Away, One Enormous Update: Shakedown Rally Comes Back With a New Car and a Rebuilt Drivetrain

    "Surpriseee! Long time no see." That is how nothke opened the first Shakedown Rally devlog in two years on August 28, having taken a break for life and other projects. The headline is Tsubasa, the game's first new car — an early-90s Group A Japanese rally machine "inspired by a few famous models," which he hopes will become the star and push the old spec17 aside. It ships only in the paid version. The under-the-hood work is bigger: a complete rewrite of the drivetrain physics, which he calls the largest internal change since the game began. Engine, clutch and tire behaviour used to be hacked in and eyeballed, with feedback forces plainly wrong and stability so poor that wheel inertias had to be set to absurd values — crash into a tree and your wheels kept spinning "like they were made of uranium," sometimes reversing direction. Now torques, power, gear ratios and inertias take real spec-sheet numbers and behave predictably, tire grip depends on vertical compression, and there's a throttle-cutting RPM limiter. The cost is that car.ini changed shape, so every existing mod is broken until its author updates it, and spec17 now accelerates hard enough that the leaderboards are effectively reset. Also in: a launcher written in Zig that finally kills the .bat files (open source, and a community member added keyboard navigation), dithering post-processing to kill colour banding on the limited palette, sound reverb and occlusion so the car muffles behind walls, per-element HUD toggles with 4K scaling, and auto-reverse when you hold the brake at a standstill — a feature he considered a skill issue until enough people asked. Particles now sort around the car's windows in two buckets so the windshield stops vanishing in a dust cloud. Built in nothke's own C++ engine, boots to driving in under three seconds; €6.99 or more for Windows, 4.7 out of 5 from 129 ratings, and the demo was deliberately not updated. Source: itch.io devlog · Game page

  • [ OPEN SOURCE ] Lancer Tactics Hands Out Its Full Source as a Modkit — 36 Mods In, 207 Bug Tickets Deep

    Olive's Godot port of the Lancer tabletop RPG added official mod support two months ago, and modders produced 36 mods in that window — first-party supplemental frames and talents, homebrew mechs, new assets, rules tweaks. Until the August 27 devlog, getting the modkit meant asking for access to a locked Discord channel, a stopgap to get it out fast while staying covered. With a lawyer-approved EULA and light source and asset watermarking now in place, the full source of Lancer Tactics ships as a 104 MB modkit download sitting on the itch.io page alongside the game builds, same 1.3.3 version. The other half of the post is a candid look at the bill for all that openness: the Trello backlog holds 207 bug tickets and 202 UI improvement tickets, the last two updates were almost entirely fixes, and the studio is hiring a part-time GDScript contractor to help dig out. Olive's diagnosis is that in-game editors plus modding opened an enormous surface area — straight Lancer-mechanics bugs are a small slice of the total, and the automated tests that cover game mechanics can't easily cover a user-facing map editor. "There's a lesson to be learned here about how deceptively much that is to bite off to chew." The map editor is staying regardless, because it is core to the appeal. The 1.3.3 changelog runs long, including an experiment letting all weapons attack setpiece obstructions and a critical module-upload fix for anyone whose mod contained dialogue. $20 or more, Windows, macOS and Linux, 4.7 out of 5 from 186 ratings; it stays close enough to the tabletop rules to import COMP/CON v2 characters. Source: itch.io devlog · Game page

  • [ TOOLS ] Pixel Composer 1.21.9 Finally Answers the Mac Thread — and the First macOS Crash Report Arrived the Same Day

    MakhamDev's node-based pixel art and VFX tool has had a community thread bluntly titled "PLZ MAKE A MAC VERSION!" running for six weeks and 22 posts. The August 25 beta opens with two words — "MacOS beta." — and the download list now carries macOS and Linux builds beside the Windows one. It was not a quiet landing: a thread reporting the macOS build crashing on startup went up within hours and is already three posts deep, which is roughly what "beta" is supposed to mean. The rest of the release is the usual dense changelog. The Canvas node has been rebuilt from scratch with most of its features stripped out and its own editor interface, on the grounds that it is now stable — old projects keep the old node. Panel dialogs open in separate windows on Windows, at the cost of losing file dropping and pen pressure, and can be turned off. New Transform Path 3D and Uniform Array nodes, shift+F5 to purge every output and re-render the scene, a library that finally searches subdirectories, and an MP4 node that now offers to download FFmpeg instead of just failing. Pixel Composer is $15 or more, MIT-licensed, built in GameMaker, comes with a Steam key, and sits at 4.9 out of 5 from 129 ratings with itch's "No generative AI was used" declaration on the page. Source: itch.io devlog · Tool page

  • [ TOOLS ] After Eight Years Free, Laigter's Author Asks His 80,000 Users Whether He Can Start Charging

    Laigter generates normal, specular, parallax and ambient occlusion maps from 2D sprites, it has been name-your-own-price on itch.io since 2019, it is GPLv3, it was an Epic MegaGrants recipient, and it is rated 5.0 out of 5 from 182 ratings. On August 24 its author azagaya posted the question directly: can he make the itch and Steam binaries paid, on an Aseprite-style model, while the source code and the GitHub build artifacts stay free? The itch downloads have quietly fallen a few versions behind the GitHub development branch because keeping both in sync stopped being sustainable. He wants to build Laigter 2.0 — data oriented, multi-threaded by design, on a custom minimal-dependency GUI library he has already started — and says plainly that he is older now, has a family to provide for, and cannot commit that much time for free. His own read on the 80,000 downloads is unsentimental: "is easy to get downloads on a free app, so no real measurement of interest to invest in a second version." A price would be the measurement. He also floats the darker possibility that with LLMs able to bypass the developer entirely, even a paid model might not work. The post ends asking for comments rather than announcing anything, and the comment thread has been answering. Source: itch.io devlog · Tool page

  • [ JAM ] Brackeys Game Jam 2026.2 Has About 35 Hours Left — Entries More Than Double to 292

    The larger of itch.io's two annual Brackeys jams opened its submission window on August 23 at 10:00 UTC and closes exactly a week later, on August 30. As always the theme dropped the moment the clock started, and this round it's Trust No One — the reveal card renders "NO" in hollow pink outline against solid cyan, and the jam's community board is now 651 posts deep. The counter reads 17.4k joined against 292 entries, up 169 in a day — more than double yesterday's 123 and the first sign of the end-of-jam flood, though it still works out to one submission for every 60 participants. The rules are the usual Brackeys set with teeth: host on itch with no external download links, any engine or framework, third-party assets allowed if you hold the licence, teams encouraged but recommended under four people — and a flat ban on AI-generated content and NSFW material. There is no grace period, so the organisers are telling people to create the itch page and upload hours early. Entries get scored by fellow submitters on Enjoyment, Gameplay, Innovation, Theme, Visuals and Audio during a two-week rating window ending September 13, and a game needs at least 20 ratings to be eligible to win — which means playing other people's entries is the price of competing. Files stay unlocked for the first 48 hours of rating for bug fixes and extra platform builds only. Hosted by Brackeys with moderators AquaXV, Yasahiro and Duxez, sponsored by web-games portal CrazyGames, with jam art based on a submission by community member Lara. Source: itch.io jam page · Entries

  • [ BUNDLE ] Games for Scholarships Blows Past $10,000 — the Goal Has Been Doubled to $20,000

    Organizer jesthehuman's back-to-school bundle collects everything submitted to the Games for Scholarships jam — 365 entries across video games, browser games and tabletop RPGs, made between July 13 and August 10 under a jam policy that barred discriminatory and AI-generated material. The resulting DRM-free package is 324 items from 159 creators for a $10 minimum, against a regular value of roughly $675. Every dollar goes to Games for Change, funding scholarships for underserved youth in the G4C Student Challenge, the nonprofit's global design competition for students aged 10 to 25. The $10,000 target it was chasing all week fell overnight, and the organiser simply moved the post: the page now reads $11,199.34 raised against a $20,000 goal, 55% funded, from 924 contributors averaging $12.12 each with a $75 top contribution. The past day alone brought in $2,643 and 218 new backers — nearly triple the previous day's pace, and by some margin the bundle's best day since it opened on August 17. It runs to September 20, so there are three more weeks to find the second $10,000. Source: itch.io bundle page · Jam page

  • AUG 28 Divinum — OUT NOW

    Ten years after development began, a solo developer working as Sigil Games has shipped it. Divinum casts you as Fjor, born under the Guardian celestial blessing, whose homeland is invaded by an outside threat; she takes up her weapon and goes looking for the ancestral legacy that might save her people. The structure is a hack-and-slash combat system laid over metroidvania exploration of an island's surface and its underground — forgotten caves, ominous swamps — hunting secrets and bosses. Two distinct primary weapons and a bank of special skills, with a fury system underneath, and the progression rejects flat numbers: bosses and hidden corners drop Runes that add unusual modifiers rather than another +5% damage, some of them carrying a downside you have to plan around. The launch post lays out a plain post-release plan — bugs, balance, player feedback, achievement localisation and more languages, then replayability content over the coming months. Early buyer reviews are recommending it on the strength of the combat animations and old-school map exploration. $14.99, cut 10% to $13.49. Windows and macOS via Steam, plus GOG, with consoles planned later. Steam · GameGrin

  • AUG 28 Allumeria — EARLY ACCESS

    Twenty months of solo development by Infinimelon put a classic voxel sandbox into Early Access at version 0.15, with the pitch that the block-building half is only the frame for an RPG progression loop — exploration, dungeon crawling and boss battles feeding a stream of new weapons, abilities, resources, enemies, structures and tools. The launch build's headline addition is NPCs and housing, built on a Comfort system that actually inspects a room: whether it's enclosed, how much light it gets, how much of that light is natural, which blocks are in it. Beds no longer skip the night outright — higher comfort makes time pass faster while you sleep — and a craftable NPC sign designates a room as somewhere a villager can move into, which they'll attempt four times a day and only if the space meets a baseline of comfort. NPCs have happiness and requirements, and won't sell you anything until you've met them. The developer's thank-you note singles out the YouTubers who kept a spotlight on the project through development and the community that "fiercely defended Allumeria's right to exist as a game," which tells you what the last year of a Minecraft-adjacent voxel game looks like from the inside. It's bundled with Lucid Blocks and heading into the PvE Survival Crafting Fest. $11.99, cut 20% to $9.59 for two weeks. Windows via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 28 Inferno Protocol — EARLY ACCESS

    Tektonic Interactive's survival horror drops one to six players into a massive maze they have to scavenge, craft and navigate their way out of, with proximity chat and walkie-talkies doing the heavy lifting on the co-op side. The maze reshuffles between nights, which is already the first thing players are arguing about on the forums — one asked what the point of chalking X marks on the walls is when the route changes underneath you. The team ran a long public demo, patched microphone selection and proximity-chat bugs through it, and pulled it from Steam an hour after launch with a thank-you note to everyone who filed bugs. Opening Early Access reviews are recommending it while being blunt about the state of things: multiplayer stability and lobby QOL need work, but reviewers who put ten hours into the demo are staying to watch it develop. $13.49, cut 20% to $10.79. Windows via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 27 Lou's Lagoon — OUT NOW

    Four years after the first prototype, Hamburg studio Tiny Roar's cozy exploration-and-crafting adventure has launched. Your Uncle Lou has disappeared and left you his seaplane and his delivery business, so you fly between the tropical islands of the Limbo archipelago running parcels, collecting resources with a vacuum-like tool called the Swirler, and rebuilding communities the storms wrecked — while the mystery of what happened to Lou unspools underneath. The studio marked the launch with a devlog that is mostly its two CEOs talking through the whole arc: where the concept came from, the inspirations, the ups and downs, and footage dug out of the archives from very early builds, alongside team members picking out what they most want players to find in the archipelago. Their own summary of the project is "our little game (that got way bigger than was ever intended)." A Deluxe Pack adds cosmetics including a bubble fan engine, and a Collector's Edition wraps in the digital artbook. Day-one bug reports are already coming in about inventory slots, and the team is answering them on the forum. $24.99, cut 10% to $22.49. PC via Steam, published by Megabit Publishing and rokaplay. Steam

  • AUG 26 Folk Emerging — OUT NOW

    The first game from Curious Dynamics, a solo developer in the EU, is a turn-based 4X that stops where every other 4X starts. Rock Paper Shotgun called it "the most intriguing 4X in a long while, partly because it declines to entirely become one"; eXplorminate put it more bluntly — "the first 5 turns of Civilization, made into a game." You lead nomadic families through the Pleistocene, and the tech tree runs from controlled fire and cooking through domestication and agriculture to megalithic construction and the first cities, at which point you are done. Underneath is a simulation that is doing more than a 4X normally bothers with: nine climates each with a dynamic food web, procedural Earth-like maps generated from real climate simulation, finite resources whose extraction damages the environment, and many generations of individually simulated people with heritable traits, visualised as a social network so you can actually read the family trees and grudges. Culture evolves its own norms that change how neighbours trade and fight with you, combat resolves as an autobattler, and narrative events can bend a tribe's whole trajectory. The demo drew 84% positive across 26 reviews and pushed the launch from July so diplomacy, combat and AI could be reworked on Next Fest feedback. Windows via Steam, and the demo ran fine under Proton. Steam · GamingOnLinux · eXplorminate

  • AUG 26 Fatal Claw — 1.0, OUT OF EARLY ACCESS

    NDEV GAMES' hand-drawn metroidvania spent nine months in Early Access and came out the other side at 94% of 135 reviews. You play Kisha, a cat resurrected by his ancestors into an underground world where Ancients and Commoners live side by side among sealed passages, stones that archive vital force, and creatures turned violent by an infection. The central loop is absorption: beat the infected and take their abilities, then use them to reach the parts of the map that were closed to you. The zones are explicitly nonlinear and systematically interconnected, the pitch being that the order you find things in becomes your version of the story, and the ending is a choice rather than a reward — you pursue whatever you decided was worth pursuing. Roughly 10 hours for the main path. The 1.0 adds new areas and content on top of everything the Early Access build accumulated. Published by Com2uS Holdings with H2 Interactive. $24.99, cut 20% for launch. Windows via Steam. Steam · Netto's Game Room · PlayDay.One

  • AUG 25 Blood Dungeon — OUT NOW

    Messhof — Mark Essen and Kristy Norindr, the two people who made Nidhogg — are back with a platforming autoshooter, and they are self-publishing it again. You run, jump and climb through dungeon arenas while the shooting happens by itself; the whole game is about positioning. Ladders, grates, chains, ceilings and the water below are all traversal surfaces, and the 100-plus enemy types are built to punish lazy routes — some fly straight through walls, others stick to them or bounce off. Kills drop Blood, which raises your level mid-run and buys upgrades, while bones go to the Dogmancer for permanent unlocks between attempts. Nine bunkmates each rewrite the movement rather than just the weapon: Leggy spins climbable webs, Bug flies, Skinja is built for speed. Six survival arenas, revealed at Day of the Devs 2026, and the soundtrack gets billing in the store copy for good reason. Opening reviews are Positive — 40 of 41. $9.99, cut 15% to $8.49. Windows and macOS via Steam, plus PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Switch and Switch 2. Steam · Game Informer

  • AUG 25 Polylithic — 1.0, OUT OF EARLY ACCESS

    Czech studio polyperfect's Stone Age survival game left Early Access on its third anniversary and change, after more than 50 updates, with the biggest content drop it has ever shipped. The premise is that humans are not yet the apex predator and you are quite far down the list: lead a tribe from desperate Paleolithic foraging to the first permanent Neolithic settlements, crafting the earliest tools in human history and researching your way up. Its visual argument is against every other prehistoric game — this one is vibrantly coloured low-poly rather than the usual brown, across tall forests, snow-dusted hills and rapids that are indifferent to your survival. Hunting runs from rabbits to mammoths. The 1.0 adds companion animals, rival tribes, expanded customisation and a comedic nature-documentary framing over the whole thing, alongside online co-op for up to 10 and a procedural Sandbox Mode. Reviews sit Mixed at 69% of 209, carried over from the Early Access years. Published by indie.io. $14.99, cut 35% to $9.74. Windows via Steam and the indie.io store. Steam · indie.io · COGconnected

  • AUG 25 Hapunan — OUT NOW (itch.io and Steam)

    Yikon Games and Neuroticfly Games' PSX-styled psychological horror went from itch.io demo to full release on both stores the same day. Its subject is balut, the Filipino midnight street food, and the vendors who walk the graveyard shift calling "Baluuuut!" into empty streets. You play Niko, a teenager two months behind on rent and facing eviction by a ruthless landlord, who goes out selling with his father to keep the family's home — and finds the trade carries secrets that belong strictly to the dark, tangled up with the name Penduko and Philippine folklore. Choices dictate who eats and who doesn't. The developers have been iterating on this in public since a 2025 Android build; the itch page has run devlogs for well over a year, and today's carries the 1.0.5 Windows build alongside the Steam launch. $3.99, cut 10% to $3.59 on Steam; $3.99 or more on itch.io. Windows. Steam · itch.io · Devlog

  • AUG 25 ASTRAL COURT DX — FREE ON STEAM

    LUA COMPUTA's arcade precision platformer opens on death row in a prison in Heaven. The Astral Court, Heaven's only court and by its own description an odd one, offers The Prisoner bail on the condition of surviving a run of deadly trials — so you platform across the Scales of Justice themselves, several of them at once, collecting stars called Shinies while the arena fills with hazards. The movement is tuned for control over commitment: you can jump even after you've already started falling, then double-jump for height, which turns every near-miss into something recoverable. Multiple characters, and a chrome-and-cloud aesthetic that looks like a late-90s console demo disc. Free, and opening Very Positive at 97% of 67 reviews. Windows via Steam. Steam
