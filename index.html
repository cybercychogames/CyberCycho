  • [ OPEN SOURCE ] Thrive 1.6 Adds Sex, Muscle and Membranes That Reach for Each Other — and Clears Its Entire Roadmap

    Revolutionary Games Studio posted Devblog #53 on August 30 to ship Thrive 1.6, the third major update to the free, open-source evolution game's Multicellular Stage. Three months on from the last one, the team says everything their public roadmap had planned up to 1.6 is now done. The namesake feature is sexual reproduction: pick it and you can no longer walk into the editor when fully grown — you have to launch a gamete cell and merge it with a gamete from another member of your species, paid back with a 20% discount on every MP cost in the editor. You can start with any one of your cell types as the gamete, then switch to anisogamy and design two, at which point you also choose which of the two "sexes" you play. Because hunting for a mate is miserable, the Signalling Agent gets a command that calls one toward you, and on Normal difficulty it will spawn one in and point it out if none exists. The other new mode, Mass Budding, lets you start the next generation as several cells instead of one via a slider — so your first cell no longer has to survive alone — at the cost of gathering more resources before you can reproduce. Sporulation was reworked to copy a chosen cell type into a dedicated spore type editable only from the reproduction tab. Alongside that: Actomyosin, a new organelle that makes "muscle cells", trading size and ATP for movement and turning speed, one per cell, improved only through specialization and adjacency — and the first organelle with a membrane restriction, since a rigid cell wall can't constrict, so it needs Normal or Double membranes. The longest-running complaint about the stage — that creatures read as a disconnected swarm rather than a body — got a fix that is not merely cosmetic: membranes of nearby cells now stretch toward each other, and the stretched sections collide, so small cells stop slipping through the gaps and getting stuck inside you. Finally, auto-evo now runs on multicellular species, so AI species mutate, migrate and diverge too, though they can't yet become multicellular on their own — the player is always first, and every AI multicellular species descends from yours. Also in: endosymbiosis for multicellular species, a reproduce hotkey, Thriveopedia search across page content and tags, and an engine bump to Godot 4.7.2. Free through the team's launcher; paying for it on Steam or itch.io is the support model, and the Steam build sits at Very Positive, 92% of 1,664 reviews. The stage is targeted for completion around the end of this year. Source: itch.io devlog · Full devblog · Release notes

  • [ DEVLOG ] A Lightgun Broke Tumor Necrosis Factor's Whole Design — Fixing It Took One Change at a Time

    ChickenHat's free Windows demo is a House of the Dead-inspired rail shooter in which you are a mutant half-worm tearing through an Earth drowning in blood, where the weak point is never the head but the red segment of the parasite coiled around each enemy. The August 30 update is mostly about supporting Sinden lightguns, and the post is candid that it "ended up being a much bigger undertaking than I thought it'd be." The problem was not the peripheral, it was the cursor. Turn the mouse pointer off and the worm whip alone gives bad shooting feedback; a larger blast effect fixed that but made distant hits read as misses, which forced a distance-based "mercy radius" so shooting felt consistent at every range, which in turn made collecting blood feel too tight, so that had to be loosened. "Rinse and repeat." The build that came out the other side extends the tutorial area into a proper first level, adds cutscenes, a Sinden border option, a standalone Aim Test scene so players can check their guns before starting, and local multiplayer — which needs two mice or lightguns and therefore rules out Steam Remote Play. Two new enemies arrive: Rebirth Cells, which appear when a Cell Type enemy eats an ATP Pod, and Infected, partially transformed nuns that drop a pod when hit in the weak spot. There's an easy mode, and the developer explains why there isn't a hard one yet — easy currently just lowers the hits needed to kill, and simply raising enemy HP for hard would make the game tedious rather than frantic, so it waits for a real ruleset change. Combat sound effects were rebuilt to include, in the post's words, "the traditional indie game pasta stirring audio." Still to come: a routing path on the results screen, a powerup shop and a level select, and then the rest of the game. Source: itch.io devlog · Demo page

  • [ DEVLOG ] Markiplier Fell Down the Clown Hole — Now It's Going to Steam, With Lamps, Brews and Breakable Walls

    Oddspeed's browser prototype about digging a mine full of clowns has been sitting on itch.io at 4.6 out of 5 from 154 ratings, and on August 28 the team — Jaco van Hemert on programming and design, 2bitdream on art — posted the reason it is about to become a real product: "super positive reception online and in our local community, plus being visited by the one and only Markiplier (crazy!)". The Steam page is up, the studio is aiming for a full release, and the update that came with the announcement is a pivot rather than a patch. Levels are now large spaces with breakable walls and several separate clown fights inside them, which broke the old lighting model outright: you now place lamps to push back the dark as you descend, and upgrading the elevator is what buys you more of them. The progression tree moved inside the level so you can spend without surfacing — the developers' own framing is that this brings it "closer to being a proper incremental game" and supports longer sessions. Worker assignment was rebuilt on player feedback: mark ore nodes, clowns and other objects, and workers distribute themselves evenly across everything marked. Clowns now drop a new resource, Clown Brews, which feeds a new Brewery building whose output confers different benefits depending on which brew you make; economy balance and building upgrade scaling were both widened, which the post notes means more clowns, "so advance with caution." Audio got the biggest outside investment — granularbiomes came on to write new music and SFX, giving every boss its own track. Free in the browser, pay-what-you-want to support it, one level of the new build playable now, and the page carries itch's "No generative AI was used" declaration. Source: itch.io devlog · Game page · Steam

  • [ TOOLS ] void◆sprite Turns Its Start Screen Into a Gallery — and Says Plainly Why It Has to Live on Discord

    voidsprite is a free, open-source pixel art editor written in C++ on SDL3 by cntrpl, ZLD1 and jelleejamm, and its selling point has always been the unglamorous stuff: pixel-perfect scaling only, indexed mode, on-canvas comments, split sessions with several files on one canvas, real-time online collaboration in beta, and an import list that reads like a museum inventory — RPG Maker 2000/2003 XYZ, Cave Story PBM, Wii TPL, Valve VTF, Atrophy Engine AETEX, and Mario Paint SRM save files. The August 29 post adds something softer. The Launchpad start screen now has a "Featured" section that shows randomly chosen user-submitted artwork, live in the latest autobuilds, and you can switch it off in settings if you would rather not see it. The stated goal is to "give pixel artists another space to showcase their work." The interesting part is the apology attached to the submission process: art is collected through the project's Discord server, and the developer knows exactly how that reads. "I know going to a random Discord server just for one thing is annoying, I myself hate the fact that it killed all forum discussions, but I don't exactly have the budget to host a web server for this, so I hope you understand." Read the pinned rules before submitting. voidsprite is still alpha, manual updates stay free forever, and the money side is a paid Windows autoupdater rather than a paid editor. Source: itch.io devlog · Tool page · GitHub

  • [ JAM ] Brackeys Game Jam 2026.2 Closed at 2,299 Entries — Tripling in the Final Half-Day

    The larger of itch.io's two annual Brackeys jams shut its submission window at 10:00 UTC on August 30, one week to the minute after opening, and the last-day flood was even steeper than usual. With eleven hours left the board read 732 entries; the final count is 2,299. Roughly two out of every three games in this jam were submitted after that snapshot, against a theme — Trust No One — that had been public since the clock started. The platform split is the clearest picture of what a browser-first jam looks like now: 1,719 entries playable in the browser, 744 Windows, 182 Linux, 91 macOS and 21 Android, with many games counted more than once. Rating opened immediately and is already moving fast — 7,891 ratings cast and 980 threads in the jam community — because only submitters can vote and a game needs at least 20 ratings to be eligible to win, which makes playing other people's entries the price of competing. Scoring runs on Enjoyment, Gameplay, Innovation, Theme, Visuals and Audio, and the window closes September 13; files stay unlocked for the first 48 hours for bug fixes and extra platform builds only. The rules that got here were the usual Brackeys set with teeth: host on itch with no external download links, any engine or framework, third-party assets allowed if you hold the licence, teams recommended under four people, and a flat ban on AI-generated content and NSFW material. Hosted by Brackeys with moderators AquaXV, Yasahiro and Duxez, sponsored by web-games portal CrazyGames, with jam art based on a submission by community member Lara. Source: itch.io jam page · Entries

  • [ BUNDLE ] Games for Scholarships Passes 1,000 Backers as the Post-Milestone Slowdown Settles In

    Organizer jesthehuman's back-to-school bundle collects everything submitted to the Games for Scholarships jam — 365 entries across video games, browser games and tabletop RPGs, made between July 13 and August 10 under a jam policy that barred discriminatory and AI-generated material. The resulting DRM-free package is 324 items from 159 creators for a $10 minimum, against a regular value of roughly $675. Every dollar goes to Games for Change, funding scholarships for underserved youth in the G4C Student Challenge, the nonprofit's global design competition for students aged 10 to 25. Two days after the original $10,000 target fell and the organiser doubled the post to $20,000, the page reads $12,754.46 raised, 63% funded, from 1,055 contributors averaging $12.08 each with a $75 top contribution. The past day added $659 and 57 backers, down again from the previous day's $896 and 74 — the third straight day of decline since the milestone spike, now running at roughly a quarter of that day's pace. The backer counter did clear 1,000. Twenty-one days left on the clock and about $7,250 to find, which at the current rate will not arrive on its own. Source: itch.io bundle page · Jam page

  • [ MILESTONE ] Sandustry Clears 100,000 Sales in Its First Week of Early Access

    Publisher Hooded Horse says Lantto Games' falling-sand factory builder passed 100,000 copies barely a week after its August 13 Early Access launch, alongside an Overwhelmingly Positive Steam rating from more than 2,000 reviews. The hook is that every pixel of the world is a real resource waiting to be mined, mixed, processed or destroyed, spread across a fully destructible open world seeded with artifacts from an ancient civilisation. It ships a native Linux build, and players have not waited around for official multiplayer — a co-op mod called SandTogether is already up on the Steam Workshop while the developer pushes out post-launch fixes at pace.   • AUG 29 2007 — OUT NOW

    Hit'n'Run Digital Studio's branching visual novel is a psychological horror built out of a very specific nostalgia: Russian online culture in the year 2007. You play Grishka, a student in G — a small science town in the Moscow region — who pulls barista shifts at a coffee shop called Moonbucks and spends the rest of his time scouring the internet for urban legends about the place he lives. Word of a girl's bizarre death sets off Brainbridge, his occult chat of choice, and the game becomes a job of separating fact from insanity out of morgue lifehacks, foreboding tarot readings, a creepy JPG and an ocean of other noise, on a clock, without losing your mind. It's nonlinear with multiple endings, has a multi-composer original soundtrack, and ships with full Russian voice acting alongside English and Russian text — a game that has clearly decided its home audience comes first. The store page is upfront that the content includes domestic and psychological violence, suicide, blood and light gore. Only one user review so far, so there's nothing to read into yet; a demo is up if you'd rather try it first. $9.99, 12 achievements, Windows via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 29 Shujaa — OUT NOW

    Shujaa is the Swahili word for hero, and Knobthorn Interactive's self-published debut is a single-player boomer shooter set in a fictional African country where a dictator has declared himself president for life and is using dark voodoo to build a supernatural army, black out the country and crush dissent. You are the unnamed upright man sent to take him down. The design is unapologetically old-school and says so: fast, unforgiving, linear, with weapons — shotgun, bolt-action rifle, rocket launcher, minigun — scattered through levels and hidden in secret areas rather than handed out, and a machete for when the ammo runs dry. Enemies split between human soldiers and supernatural things with different attacks and one shared objective. Levels are loosely modelled on real places and gated the 1993 way, by keys, switches, ambushes and environmental hazards. No user reviews yet — it went up yesterday. $3.99, cut 10% to $3.59. Windows and Linux via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 28 House 2 — OUT NOW

    Six years after the 2020 original, Bark Bark Games' sequel is out, published by Glowstick Entertainment. Tabby is trapped in the same never-ending nightmare while a storm rages outside, and the curse is coming from inside the walls; you either uncover it or you succumb. The headline change is cosmetic and total — the first House was retro pixel art, this one is a hand-drawn storybook, which mostly serves to make the deaths worse, since dying "in gruesomely elaborate ways" is an advertised feature. The house rearranges itself in real time in response to what you do, the puzzles are non-linear and want you to experiment with items rather than follow a chain, there are multiple endings, a deadly time-loop you are racing, and a final choice about whether to save or slay your family — with the store copy immediately undercutting itself: "Or is it?" There is also an in-game console, the BarkStation, loaded with PS1-styled minigames including a Kimiko's Curse 2. Opening reviews are Positive at 85% of 33. $19.99, cut 10% to $17.99. Windows via Steam; consoles follow on September 26. Steam · Bloody Disgusting

  • AUG 28 Divinum — OUT NOW

    Ten years after development began, a solo developer working as Sigil Games has shipped it. Divinum casts you as Fjor, born under the Guardian celestial blessing, whose homeland is invaded by an outside threat; she takes up her weapon and goes looking for the ancestral legacy that might save her people. The structure is a hack-and-slash combat system laid over metroidvania exploration of an island's surface and its underground — forgotten caves, ominous swamps — hunting secrets and bosses. Two distinct primary weapons and a bank of special skills, with a fury system underneath, and the progression rejects flat numbers: bosses and hidden corners drop Runes that add unusual modifiers rather than another +5% damage, some of them carrying a downside you have to plan around. The launch post lays out a plain post-release plan — bugs, balance, player feedback, achievement localisation and more languages, then replayability content over the coming months. Early buyer reviews are recommending it on the strength of the combat animations and old-school map exploration. $14.99, cut 10% to $13.49. Windows and macOS via Steam, plus GOG, with consoles planned later. Steam · GameGrin

  • AUG 28 Allumeria — EARLY ACCESS

    Twenty months of solo development by Infinimelon put a classic voxel sandbox into Early Access at version 0.15, with the pitch that the block-building half is only the frame for an RPG progression loop — exploration, dungeon crawling and boss battles feeding a stream of new weapons, abilities, resources, enemies, structures and tools. The launch build's headline addition is NPCs and housing, built on a Comfort system that actually inspects a room: whether it's enclosed, how much light it gets, how much of that light is natural, which blocks are in it. Beds no longer skip the night outright — higher comfort makes time pass faster while you sleep — and a craftable NPC sign designates a room as somewhere a villager can move into, which they'll attempt four times a day and only if the space meets a baseline of comfort. NPCs have happiness and requirements, and won't sell you anything until you've met them. The developer's thank-you note singles out the YouTubers who kept a spotlight on the project through development and the community that "fiercely defended Allumeria's right to exist as a game," which tells you what the last year of a Minecraft-adjacent voxel game looks like from the inside. It's bundled with Lucid Blocks and heading into the PvE Survival Crafting Fest. $11.99, cut 20% to $9.59 for two weeks. Windows via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 28 Inferno Protocol — EARLY ACCESS

    Tektonic Interactive's survival horror drops one to six players into a massive maze they have to scavenge, craft and navigate their way out of, with proximity chat and walkie-talkies doing the heavy lifting on the co-op side. The maze reshuffles between nights, which is already the first thing players are arguing about on the forums — one asked what the point of chalking X marks on the walls is when the route changes underneath you. The team ran a long public demo, patched microphone selection and proximity-chat bugs through it, and pulled it from Steam an hour after launch with a thank-you note to everyone who filed bugs. Opening Early Access reviews are recommending it while being blunt about the state of things: multiplayer stability and lobby QOL need work, but reviewers who put ten hours into the demo are staying to watch it develop. $13.49, cut 20% to $10.79. Windows via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 27 Lou's Lagoon — OUT NOW

    Four years after the first prototype, Hamburg studio Tiny Roar's cozy exploration-and-crafting adventure has launched. Your Uncle Lou has disappeared and left you his seaplane and his delivery business, so you fly between the tropical islands of the Limbo archipelago running parcels, collecting resources with a vacuum-like tool called the Swirler, and rebuilding communities the storms wrecked — while the mystery of what happened to Lou unspools underneath. The studio marked the launch with a devlog that is mostly its two CEOs talking through the whole arc: where the concept came from, the inspirations, the ups and downs, and footage dug out of the archives from very early builds, alongside team members picking out what they most want players to find in the archipelago. Their own summary of the project is "our little game (that got way bigger than was ever intended)." A Deluxe Pack adds cosmetics including a bubble fan engine, and a Collector's Edition wraps in the digital artbook. Day-one bug reports are already coming in about inventory slots, and the team is answering them on the forum. $24.99, cut 10% to $22.49. PC via Steam, published by Megabit Publishing and rokaplay.
