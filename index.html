  • [ OPEN SOURCE ] Bitsy's Creator Just Open-Sourced the Game Boy Advance Engine Behind WITH THE LAST MOONBEAM

    Adam Le Doux — the person who made Bitsy, the little engine that launched a thousand tiny games — posted a four-word devlog on August 3: "moonbeamkit, the game engine i made for WITH THE LAST MOONBEAM, is now open source! <3" The code is up on Codeberg. moonbeamkit is the GBA engine powering his 2023 knyttlike platformer, a 1.3 MB ROM you can download free or play in-browser through webretro, sitting at 4.7/5 from 37 ratings. His companion blog post is refreshingly honest about what you're getting: moonbeamkit is "a lot more complex" than his other GBA engine and expects a specific pipeline — Aseprite for art, Tiled for maps, Twine for dialogue — so he frames it as an educational resource more than a drop-in toolkit. That other engine, tchotchke, is the opposite extreme: a minimal bitsy-like of tiles, rooms and doors that he wrote on his phone, contained in a single C file, needing nothing but a text editor and a GBA toolchain. Source: itch.io devlog · Two GBA game engines · Codeberg

  • [ DEVLOG ] Baldi's Basics Plus 1.0 Is on Schedule — and Still Won't Say Who's Publishing It

    Basically Games posted its August status update on the Early Access edutainment-parody roguelike, and the news is mostly the absence of bad news: development of the 1.0 build is running to plan. Having finished the horizontal slice last time out — every important feature at a "placeholder sufficient" state — the work now is dragging each of those features to a finished one, which means "a lot of programming and game design work" against a schedule laid out with Nayan that runs to the end of this year, and which is explicitly not the whole schedule. Finalized audio and visual assets come after, and "that's also an area where a publisher should be able to help out quite a bit." On that front: still no announcement, but "there has been even more movement in this area since the last status update." Versions 0.14.3, 0.14.4 and 0.14.5 have shipped to Early Access in the meantime and 0.14 will keep getting occasional bugfix passes. So much of 1.0 is under wraps that the update's one screenshot has its spoilers painted over in red blocks — which the dev isn't worried about, since "so many of [your guesses] are so far from correct." Also announced: a Makeship plushie of Playtime, deliberately bald on the back of her head to match her scribbly MS-Paint sprite, already at 75% of its goal and only orderable until August 23. Source: itch.io devlog

  • [ DEVLOG ] Two Years Silent, NeuraQuarium Returns With the Update That Unblocks It

    urocyongames' neural-net evolution sim — critters with visible, editable brains swimming around eating each other — had painted itself into a corner. "Every new feature added more overhead, dragging performance," the developer writes, and years of accumulated tech debt were "ossifying the codebase" until experiments stopped taking and real-job work took over. v1.23 is the fix: brains, scent and heat dispersal, vision, taxonomy rendering and save/load have all been rewritten to behave identically but far faster, and the headroom went straight into scale — bigger maximum levels and a critter population cap raised from 750 to 1,500. The neural net renderer now draws curved synapses that animate their signal by type and fade everything but the hovered neuron's connections; a visual-field overlay shows a selected critter's vision range and current foveal focus. The taxonomy tree got elbow-style ancestry lines, filters for relatives of living species, a zoomed-in stat breakdown and an AutoTag feature that names significant branches for you. Old saves still load. And after years of the tiny green dots being called "fruit," they are now, correctly, ALGAE. $4.95, Windows, macOS and Linux. Source: itch.io devlog

  • [ DEVLOG ] Three Years in the Making: Fire on the Mountain Build 1 Goes Live

    Nikko pushed the first public build of their Ren'Py visual novel to itch.io on August 2 — 38 CGs, 2 minigames, 32 original songs and roughly 40,000 words. You play Ripley, head supervisor of the chronically understaffed Campsite 7 in Crestfall National Park, in a dark comedy that the page is upfront about: mature subject matter including drugs, violence and suicidal ideation, but explicitly SFW — "no graphic nudity, simulated or described sex, etc." The story is linear and the choices are deliberately inconsequential, so nobody is grinding affinity; the one thing worth watching for is the free-time segments, where a character icon turning green means there's more conversation to be had. The in-game phone's Messages, Forums, Web and Notes tabs are still stubbed out in this build. "This has been a labor of love that I've worked on here and there for the past 3 years," Nikko writes, adding a note that the Visa/Mastercard squeeze on adult games makes support matter more than usual — "Our games will remain free, as always." Name-your-own-price, Windows, macOS and Android, about 420 MB each, no generative AI. Source: itch.io devlog · Game page

  • [ COMMUNITY ] Seventeen Designers, One Chain: The Snake That Eats Puzzle Game Mechanics Tops itch.io's Featured Row

    Riding itch.io's featured shelf right now is a free browser collection built the way you'd play exquisite corpse. Seventeen members of the Thinky Puzzle Games Discord — Tetramouse, xxuurruuii, HKMG, maia s ravn, Osi Fisher, Aspeon, Slabdrill, configurations, Monakrom, pluf, CHz, clickmazes, Pastimeshark, Mistafixxa, bird, dead0ne and knexator — passed a single-screen PuzzleScript game down the line between January and July 2026. Each participant received "a small piece of the previous participant's puzzle (either the sprites, or the shape of the level)" and had to build something new out of it, so two neighbouring puzzles may share a tileset while the objects mean something entirely different, or keep the level's silhouette while a new context rewrites what you're doing in it. The games are presented in the order they were made rather than by difficulty, so the collection explicitly invites you to skip around; Z undoes, R restarts, Esc returns to level select. Free, in-browser, and sitting at a clean 5.0. Source: itch.io · Thinky Games

  • [ BUNDLE ] Game Industry Hardship Fund: 127 Games for $10, Now Past $87K

    Necrosoft Games (Demonschool) is hosting an itch.io bundle with 109 other creators to bankroll the United Videogame Workers–CWA Local 9433's hardship fund, which pays cash to laid-off and under-employed game developers across the US and Canada — two application tiers, under $1,000 and $1,000–$5,000, with roughly $25,000 already distributed to 30 applicants out of union dues before the bundle existed. 127 DRM-free items normally worth about $1,023 go for $10: A Short Hike, SkateBIRD, Cook, Serve, Delicious!, They Bleed Pixels, Wheels of Aurelia, Lucah: Born of a Dream, NEON STRUCT and more. Live since July 30 and running through August 13, it has pulled $87,471.12 from 6,184 contributors — 87% of a $100,000 goal — at an average of $14.14 each. The organizers' framing is blunt: many applicants "have lost housing, are on food assistance, or have put off necessary medical treatment." Source: itch.io · Aftermath

  • [ PLATFORM ] itch App Finally Understands Bundles — Plus %command% Launch Args

    Two app updates this month, v26.15.0 and v26.17.0, fix one of the platform's oldest annoyances: bundles you own now appear in your library as their own pages, sortable, searchable and filterable, and installable without claiming each game on the website first. leafo explains why it took work — the Bundle for Racial Justice and Equality held 1,741 items and sold over 800,000 times, so minting a download key per item per buyer would have meant a billion database rows; ownership is stored as a single record instead, and the app now reads that directly. Also landing: per-game Launch settings for sandboxing, network access and environment variables, a long-requested %command% pattern for wrapping launches in a compatibility or debug tool, the ability to reveal and install downloads not tagged for your OS, a local-first sidebar search, and per-profile play-time tracking. Source: itch.io blog

  • [ JAM ] LOWREZJAM 2026 Now Open — 64×64 Pixels, Hard Ceiling

    itch.io's long-running resolution-constraint jam is under way: build a game that renders at 64×64 pixels or less. Scaling the window up for display is fine, the game view is not — and hosts JackOatley and Gurpreet Singh Matharoo have zero patience for negotiation on that point: "Can I go higher than the 64x64 resolution? Submissions run August 1–16, community voting through August 23, and there is nothing to win — "Only the enjoyment of friendly competition within a great community." 330 jammers have signed up, with the first 4 entries already in. Source: itch.io

  • AUG 5 Fields of Mistria — 1.0 ON AUGUST 5

    Two years to the day after it hit Early Access, NPC Studio's pixel farming RPG gets its finished form. The Chicago studio — co-directed by Claire Belton and Andrew Duff, who created Pusheen before this — put out a game that cleared 100,000 downloads in its first week and 250,000 in its first month on the strength of an art style and soundtrack that had people arguing it out-farmed Stardew Valley. Version 1.0 finally closes the loops it shipped with: relationship caps rise to 10 hearts, bringing proposals, wedding events, marriage and children; the town-repair storyline becomes completable; Renown goes to level 100; and there are new dungeon quests and rewards, skill perks, furniture, cosmetics, mount skins and achievements. Beta localizations land in Simplified and Traditional Chinese, French, Japanese, Korean, Russian and Spanish. The promise is that "the game and storylines will be completable as originally envisioned." PC only at $13.99 — consoles are a maybe, and only after launch. Steam · NPC Studio announcement

  • AUG 4 Big Walk — LAUNCHES TODAY

    Six years after Untitled Goose Game, House House unlocks its follow-up today at 9am ET / 1pm UTC, published by Panic. It's a cooperative "walker-talker": a wide-open bushland world you cross with 2 to 12 friends — "i.e. a focused co-op experience" at the low end, "i.e. an absolute mess" at the high end — where the real puzzle is staying in contact. Proximity voice chat fades voices over distance, echoes them down corridors, muffles them through walls and crackles them through walkie-talkies; when talking isn't an option you're left pointing, waving and flailing. The toybox runs from laser pointers, binoculars and megaphones to flares, whiteboards, cowbells and "a big golden head (???)". Crossplay is in, and unlike most co-op sandboxes it's hand-crafted with an actual ending. The Melbourne studio isn't relaxed about it: designer Nico Disseldorp says he's "terrified" — "just the fact that it's been so long means you want the investment to have been worth it" — while Stuart Gillespie-Cook explains the overrun simply, "Big Walk kind of had a scope of its own." PC (Steam, Epic), PS5 and Switch 2, plus day-one on PS Plus Essential through August 31. Steam · GoNintendo interview · PlayStation LifeStyle

  • AUG 3 Crown Of Silence — Early Access

    Nyro Interactive's self-published 2D Metroidvania went into Early Access on August 3, and it wears its Souls-like influences openly: colossal bosses with fixed attack patterns, an emphasis on patience over reflexes, and a story told through crumbled statues and background detail rather than cutscenes — "every ruin whispers a fragment of a past tragedy." Rather than locking you into a class, it hands you weapons, armour and magical artifacts and expects you to re-equip per enemy, swapping between breaking guards with heavy strikes and dodging out of reach. The labyrinth underneath is classic genre construction: a silenced gothic kingdom that opens up as you find the tools to get deeper into it. Steam · GameGrin

  • AUG 3 Kin and Conquest — OUT NOW

    The Fox Knocks shipped 1.0 of its incremental action-adventure simultaneously on itch.io and Steam, ending a run of public 0.5.x builds — "Kin and Conquest is officially released," reads the entire devlog. The Evil Geomancer has taken the mountain and conjured fiends from across the land to hold it; the sacred Evertree picks you to go in. You guide a fox with nothing but the mouse cursor — keyboard and gamepad optional — and combat resolves by proximity, your fox swinging automatically whenever something gets close, so the play is positioning and dodging rather than clicking attacks. Progression stacks equipment, upgrades, a skill tree and permanent Blessings on top of each other. $8.99 on Steam with a 10% launch discount (co-published with Gamersky Games), or $9.99 name-your-own-price on itch.io; ten languages at launch. Steam · itch.io devlog

  • JUL 31 Sephiria — 1.0 OUT NOW

  • JUL 31 The Relic: First Guardian — OUT NOW

    Six years of work by a core team of about a dozen at Project Cloud Games, out digitally via Perp Games on PC and PS5 (Xbox Series and Switch 2 later this year, physical PS5 on September 4). You're the last guardian of the apocalyptic realm of Arsilthus, recovering lost memories — Relics — to restore a broken world. The souls-like twist is that stamina is reserved strictly for defence and dodging, so attacking costs you nothing; traditional level-ups are gone too, replaced by 70+ collectible Relics with distinct passive effects to build around. The launch has been bumpy on the technical side: the studio shipped two patches on day one and a third the same evening, saying its "current focus [is] fully on fixing technical issues and performance." A Night of the Unextinguished Flame DLC pack is out alongside it. Steam · Perp Games dev update

  • JUL 31 Corsair Cove — OUT NOW

    Limbic Entertainment (Tropico 6, Park Beyond) and Hooded Horse turn the city builder sideways — or rather upright. Your pirate haven doesn't sprawl outward across a deserted island, it climbs: buildings cling to cliff faces, stitched together by bridges, ropes and walkways until the whole thing grows into a vertical town, with production chains covering 50+ goods feeding the harbor and fleet below. Four progression paths — Notoriety, Empire, Seafaring and Wealth — unlock their own missions, buildings and ships. GameStar's Maurice Weber, four hours into the demo, called it "one of the most original city-builders in a long time," with long-term depth the open question.
