  • [ ASSETS ] Kenney Game Assets All-in-1 Hits v3.7 With a Domino Pack, Extra Patterns and Rebuilt City Roads

    Kenney has been handing out coherent, engine-agnostic game art for over a decade, and the paid All-in-1 bundle — every one of those freely available packs in a single 60,000-plus asset download — took its August 22 update. Version 3.7 adds a Domino Pack and a Pattern Pack Extra, and reworks three existing sets: City Kit: Roads gains traffic lights, road signs and more, the Crosshair Pack picks up a glow effect and separate vector files, and Mini Dungeon gets five new models alongside improvements to the rest. The bundle spans 2D sprites, 3D models, UI elements, 1,200-plus sound effects and music loops, and bitmap and vector fonts, shipped as PNG, SVG, OBJ, FBX, GLTF and OGG. An optional Windows and Linux launcher throws in utilities: a store thumbnail generator for Steam, itch.io and the Nintendo eShop, tilesheet splitting and merging, vector-to-bitmap conversion, lossless PNG compression and audio processing. $19.95 or more, usable in unlimited commercial projects with no attribution required, and new packs stay free for existing owners. One caveat from the changelog — don't overwrite an older install, remove the old files first. Source: itch.io devlog · Asset page

  • [ TOOLS ] Decker 1.70 Polishes the HyperCard-Style Toolkit That Saves to a Single HTML File

    Internet Janitor's free, open-source "holistic medium and toolkit for digital arts and crafts" — part paint program, part presentation tool, part database, part game engine, all wearing a 1-bit Macintosh face — shipped a maintenance release on August 21. Decks save as one self-contained .html file you can drop on a website or publish straight to itch, and the whole environment also runs in the browser. 1.70 is mostly repairs: the web build's read[] and write[] dialogs no longer misfire on a brief touchpad tap, the native build stops crashing on alert[] boxes with very large fonts or text, Contraption instances now correctly pick up changes to their prototype scripts, and "Snap to Grid" finally behaves sensibly with the box, rectangle and ellipse tools. The new features are small but pointed — resource lists for sounds, fonts and prototypes are sorted alphabetically instead of by order of appearance in the deck, the Font/DA Mover gains a "Script..." button for reading and editing module scripts, and the Gamepad Interface accepts WASD as an alias for the cursor keys so keyboard-plus-mouse games are easier to build. Windows, macOS and Linux, with source on GitHub and a scripting language, Lil, of its own. Source: itch.io devlog · Tool page

  • [ TOOLS ] 1BITDRAGON 5.0 Lands With a Built-In Synth, Custom WAV Import and Per-Track Stem Export

    The itch.io music tool built for developers who, in its own words, "have no clue about music" shipped its biggest version yet this morning. 1BITDRAGON's pitch is loop-perfect, royalty-free tracks drawn on a grid — no DAW, no plugins, no music theory — and 5.0 widens what fits on that grid: 8 instrument tracks, and 50 blocks per song instead of 30, with adjustable block lengths and a choice of 4/4 or 3/4 time signatures. The headline addition is Dragon Synth, a built-in synthesizer with 15 effects, alongside support for importing your own WAV files on top of the stock 223 handcrafted instruments, 275 drum sounds and 210 accompaniment patterns. Export gets a serious upgrade too — offline rendering plus per-track separation, so you can pull individual stems out for mixing elsewhere. Workflow picks up box selection for notes and built-in video tutorials. Songs made in 4.3 still open. $29.99 with a Steam key included; Windows, macOS (Universal, Intel and Apple Silicon) and Linux builds are all live, and the developer is specifically asking Apple Silicon users for feedback. Source: itch.io devlog · Tool page

  • [ RELEASE ] After a Nine-Day Delay, Buried Below Is Out — "The Tape Is Playing Again"

    Aura Studios' PSX/VHS survival horror landed on August 21 on both Steam and itch.io, closing out a year and a half of development and a launch that slipped from August 12 when Steam's approval process ran long. An old tape replays your worst memory and you take control as it runs: a camping trip, a storm, a girlfriend gone missing near an abandoned mine you have to scavenge tools to breach. Built in Unreal, it ships with subtitles, colourblind-friendly options and configurable controls, at $4.99 with a 15% launch discount taking it to $4.24. The solo-led project credits a long list of collaborators — artists, voice actors, musicians, playtesters — and the dev signed off with "Every download, review, video, stream, comment and message helps us continue creating games like this. From gamers, for gamers." Source: itch.io devlog · Steam · Game page

  • [ BUNDLE ] 324 Games for $10: itch.io's Games for Scholarships Charity Bundle Is Live Through September 20

    Organizer jesthehuman's back-to-school bundle collects everything submitted to the Games for Scholarships jam — 365 entries across video games, browser games and tabletop RPGs, made between July 13 and August 10 under a jam policy that barred discriminatory and AI-generated material. The resulting DRM-free package is 324 items from 159 creators for a $10 minimum, against a regular value of roughly $674. Every dollar goes to Games for Change, funding scholarships for underserved youth in the G4C Student Challenge, the nonprofit's global design competition for students aged 10 to 25. Collection opened August 17 and runs to September 20; as of this morning it has cleared the halfway mark, raising $5,101 of a $10,000 goal — 51% funded — from 428 contributors, who are averaging $11.91 each, comfortably above the asking price. That is roughly $1,080 and 90 more backers added in the past day alone, with the largest single contribution standing at $30. Source: itch.io bundle page · Jam page

  • [ MILESTONE ] Sandustry Clears 100,000 Sales in Its First Week of Early Access

    Publisher Hooded Horse says Lantto Games' falling-sand factory builder passed 100,000 copies barely a week after its August 13 Early Access launch, alongside an Overwhelmingly Positive Steam rating from more than 2,000 reviews. The hook is that every pixel of the world is a real resource waiting to be mined, mixed, processed or destroyed, spread across a fully destructible open world seeded with artifacts from an ancient civilisation. It ships a native Linux build, and players have not waited around for official multiplayer — a co-op mod called SandTogether is already up on the Steam Workshop while the developer pushes out post-launch fixes at pace. Source: GamingOnLinux · Steam

  • AUG 21 Social Democracy: An Alternate History — FREE ON STEAM

    Autumn Chen's itch.io interactive fiction — a quality-based narrative card game built on the DendryNexus engine, itself descended from the StoryNexus tech behind Fallen London — arrived on Steam at no charge. You run the SPD from 1928 to 1934 and try to keep the NSDAP out of power: play cards for party and government actions across 70-plus historical and alternate-historical events, court the Communists or the Centre, choose between a radical economic plan, welfare spending or unwinding the Versailles indemnities, and fall back on paramilitaries when nothing else works — though yours may be too weak and demoralised to matter. Unemployment, inflation, support for the republic and party standing across six demographics all shift underneath you. Originally made for Spring Thing 2024 and still free on itch.io, where it holds 4.8 out of 5 from 247 ratings; the Steam debut opened Very Positive. Windows and Linux via Steam; browser, Windows, macOS, Linux and Android on itch.io. Steam · itch.io

  • AUG 21 The Sorting Bureau — OUT NOW

    Apelsinka Games' cozy sorting sim hands you a desk covered in someone's life — vintage buttons, foreign coins, delicate stamps, old gears, forgotten sweets — and asks you to work out where every item belongs. There are no timers, penalties or scores. The appeal is tactile ASMR sound design, where metal clinks, fabric rustles and wood thuds as each piece snaps into its container, and the clients whose boxes you sort tell their stories through the objects alone: a watchmaker and his gears, a pastry chef and her moulds, a manga artist buried in brushes and sketches. New boxes, clients and sorting rules turn up every morning across dozens of themed desks. Very Positive from its opening reviews. $6.99, cut 30% to $4.89 for launch. Windows and macOS via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 21 VHOLUME — OUT NOW

    A melancholic first-person parkour adventure set in a dystopian brutalist city, where an errand you were sent to run turns into an odyssey through the machinery of bureaucracy. Momentum-based traversal carries you across the concrete of a derelict capital administered by an authoritarian Ministry, with environmental puzzles between the running and vaulting. Built by Niels Tiercelin, Nathan Grange and Léonard Lemaitre and published by IronEqual, off a demo that has been up since February. $8.00, discounted 10% to $7.20 for launch week. PC via Steam. Steam · Finger Guns

  • AUG 20 How to Fish — OUT NOW

    Dazed Games' 1-4 player physics fishing sim opens with a drunken boating accident that strands you on a small island, and the long job of earning your way home. You start with no gear or weapons at all: fish fish, kill fish, sell fish, buy better rods and weapons, take quests and boss fights to unlock more dangerous islands with better loot, hunt the rare variant of every species, land trick shots for bigger payouts and gamble the proceeds for more. Online co-op, full controller support and Steam achievements are all in. It shot straight onto Steam's top-seller charts — Very Positive from more than 6,700 reviews inside three days. $7.99, discounted 38% to $4.95. PC via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 20 Puppergeist — OUT NOW

    Serenity Forge and Mecrew Games' rhythm visual novel sends young witch Claire into the Doggie Underworld to find her lost Pup, picking up a demon named Lunne along the way. The dog spirits she meets each carry a story of friendship, love or loss, and lifting their moods happens through Rhythm Heaven-style minigames rather than dialogue trees. Pushed back from July 16 to land across four storefronts at once; currently sitting at 96% positive from 52 Steam reviews. $4.99. PC via Steam and Epic Games Store, Nintendo Switch and Xbox. Steam · COGconnected

  • AUG 20 Mortal Shell II — OUT NOW

    Cold Symmetry's standalone sequel to its 2020 soulslike breakout unlocked on August 20 at 10:00 Pacific: possess warrior Shells, dethrone false gods and drag a ravaged world back from ruin through high-stakes, adrenaline-forward combat. Devout Edition buyers have been in since August 17 on 72 hours of advanced access, and the pre-launch open beta pulled more than a million players across Steam, PS5 and Xbox. $49.99 standard / $59.99 Devout, with a physical PS5 Revered Edition for collectors. PC, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. Steam · DLCompare

  • AUG 20 Mexican Ninja — OUT NOW

    Bucharest's Amber Studio sends its 2.5D roguelike beat 'em up out across PC and consoles on August 20. Nuevo Tokyo is run by the Narkuzas — a feudal ruling class formed when Mexico's narcos and Japan's yakuza merged — and you join the rebels plotting against its five clan heads. Combat leans on precise movement, positioning and timing, layered with spirit powers and outlandish "Mexican Jutsus," while randomized runs, meta-upgrades and a skill tree called the Way of the Donkey carry progress between deaths. A free demo remains up on Steam. PC via Steam, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. Steam · Saving Content

  • AUG 19 Project P.I.T.T. — OUT NOW

    Solo developer Froke's incremental physics-automation sandbox puts you in a brutalist PSX-styled corporate facility with one task: manufacture rubber ducks and feed them to a bottomless pit. Production escalates from there into absurd contraptions built out of magnets, fans and other workplace-approved equipment, chasing corporate targets and colossal combos while the Maw grows and the question of who exactly you work for goes unanswered. The game racked up 10 million-plus YouTube views on organic creator coverage and neared 75,000 wishlists before launch; published by Pretty Soon, also behind FEED IT and Backpack Hero. 15% off for the first two weeks. PC via Steam. Steam · GamingOnLinux

  • AUG 19 Hearth and Hamlet — OUT NOW

    Phorust Studios' cozy incremental citybuilder starts you at a single campfire and grows it into a full medieval kingdom: assign workers to gather resources, build up from market stalls and town halls to barracks, banks and magic academies, and lean on deep upgrade trees for a relaxing idle-management loop. Published by Runic Forge off a free Steam demo that drew more than 100,000 lifetime players, with the game itself passing 150,000 wishlists ahead of launch. PC via Steam. Steam · GAMESTIC

  • AUG 18 Last Pirates: Die Together — Early Access

    Ukrainian studio RetroStyle Games' 1-4 player co-op horror comedy leans on chaotic ragdoll physics and ridiculously stretchy arms: haul as much booty as you can back to your captain while monsters — and the same teamwork keeping you alive — turn your own crewmates into hazards. Entered Steam Early Access off a demo that pulled 300,000-plus players and nearly 200,000 wishlists. PC via Steam. Steam · GamingBible
