  • [ TOOLS ] Pixel Composer 1.21.9 Finally Answers the Mac Thread — and the First macOS Crash Report Arrived the Same Day

    MakhamDev's node-based pixel art and VFX tool has had a community thread bluntly titled "PLZ MAKE A MAC VERSION!" running for six weeks and 22 posts. The August 25 beta opens with two words — "MacOS beta." — and the download list now carries macOS and Linux builds beside the Windows one. It was not a quiet landing: a thread reporting the macOS build crashing on startup went up within hours and is already three posts deep, which is roughly what "beta" is supposed to mean. The rest of the release is the usual dense changelog. The Canvas node has been rebuilt from scratch with most of its features stripped out and its own editor interface, on the grounds that it is now stable — old projects keep the old node. Panel dialogs open in separate windows on Windows, at the cost of losing file dropping and pen pressure, and can be turned off. New Transform Path 3D and Uniform Array nodes, shift+F5 to purge every output and re-render the scene, a library that finally searches subdirectories, and an MP4 node that now offers to download FFmpeg instead of just failing. Pixel Composer is $15 or more, MIT-licensed, built in GameMaker, comes with a Steam key, and sits at 4.9 out of 5 from 129 ratings with itch's "No generative AI was used" declaration on the page. Source: itch.io devlog · Tool page

  • [ TOOLS ] After Eight Years Free, Laigter's Author Asks His 80,000 Users Whether He Can Start Charging

    Laigter generates normal, specular, parallax and ambient occlusion maps from 2D sprites, it has been name-your-own-price on itch.io since 2019, it is GPLv3, it was an Epic MegaGrants recipient, and it is rated 5.0 out of 5 from 182 ratings. On August 24 its author azagaya posted the question directly: can he make the itch and Steam binaries paid, on an Aseprite-style model, while the source code and the GitHub build artifacts stay free? The itch downloads have quietly fallen a few versions behind the GitHub development branch because keeping both in sync stopped being sustainable. He wants to build Laigter 2.0 — data oriented, multi-threaded by design, on a custom minimal-dependency GUI library he has already started — and says plainly that he is older now, has a family to provide for, and cannot commit that much time for free. His own read on the 80,000 downloads is unsentimental: "is easy to get downloads on a free app, so no real measurement of interest to invest in a second version." A price would be the measurement. He also floats the darker possibility that with LLMs able to bypass the developer entirely, even a paid model might not work. The post ends asking for comments rather than announcing anything, and the comment thread has been answering. Source: itch.io devlog · Tool page

  • [ DEMO ] Atelier Sentô Return to itch.io With a Complete Remake of The Doll Shop

    The French comic-artist duo behind The Coral Cave put up a demo on August 25 for a ground-up rebuild of The Doll Shop, a game that started life as a free prototype made during a masterclass with students. New artwork, new soundtrack, new dialogue — plus places, characters and endings that weren't in the original. You play a quiet, secretive dollmaker in a snowbound village in the Japanese countryside; his childhood friend comes back, and he has to choose between the dolls he loves and the chance at a life outside them. It sits between adventure game and visual novel: wander the village, go into homes and shops, talk to locals with dialogue choices that carry consequences, repair creepy dolls and return them to their owners, visit the shrine, soak in the public bath, work through every ramen recipe at the restaurant. Three endings, with a hidden fourth for finding all of them. Atelier Sentô describe the tone as "a strange comfort in its horror and twisted charm in its romance" — no jump scares, violence kept off-screen and no less frightening for it. Every detail is hand-drawn, drawn from the northern Japanese landscapes where the pair used to live, and the page carries itch's "No generative AI was used" declaration. The itch listing already holds 4.7 out of 5 from 449 ratings and an itch.io Recommends badge. The demo is a 270 MB Windows download, English, French, Japanese and Simplified Chinese, about half an hour long; the full game is aimed at October. Source: itch.io devlog · Demo page · Steam

  • [ PORTS ] A GMTK Jam Entry That Runs on the Wii and the Dreamcast: BELTRUNNER Adds Windows and Fits Every Build on a Floppy

    Matt Sephton's GMTK Game Jam 2026 submission has spent the month since the jam sprawling outward instead of onto a second project. BELTRUNNER is an Asteroids-shaped arcade game about clearing asteroid belt A573-R01-D5 where you cannot actually die — the timer just runs down, and everything you do is about feeding the countdown across 16 waves. The August 25 update fixed score saving on the platforms where it was broken, added it to the ones that never had it, shipped a Windows build requiring OpenGL, and squeezed the zips further. That last part is the point: the Windows build is 785 kB, the PortMaster handheld build 711 kB, the Dreamcast 866 kB, the Wii 982 kB, macOS 1.2 MB — every one of them fits on a floppy disk, which was its own devlog five days earlier. The port list came together in stages through August, Wii and Linux handhelds first, then macOS and Sega Dreamcast. It is built in Jinks, Sephton's own engine, which he calls "a totally new way to make games" — the multi-platform reach is the argument for it. Web version free forever and rated 5.0 from four ratings; $5 or more buys the downloads, with Wiimote and Joy-Con among the supported inputs and colour-blind friendly rendering. Source: itch.io devlog · Game page

  • [ JAM ] Brackeys Game Jam 2026.2 Enters Its Final Three Days — 123 Entries In, Theme "Trust No One"

    The larger of itch.io's two annual Brackeys jams opened its submission window on August 23 at 10:00 UTC and closes exactly a week later, on August 30. As always the theme dropped the moment the clock started, and this round it's Trust No One — the reveal card renders "NO" in hollow pink outline against solid cyan, and the jam's community board is now 607 posts deep. The counter reads 17.5k joined against 123 entries, up 41 in a day and half again as many as the day before, but still one submission for every 140 participants with three days to go — the real flood always arrives in the last hours. The rules are the usual Brackeys set with teeth: host on itch with no external download links, any engine or framework, third-party assets allowed if you hold the licence, teams encouraged but recommended under four people — and a flat ban on AI-generated content and NSFW material. There is no grace period, so the organisers are telling people to create the itch page and upload hours early. Entries get scored by fellow submitters on Enjoyment, Gameplay, Innovation, Theme, Visuals and Audio during a two-week rating window ending September 13, and a game needs at least 20 ratings to be eligible to win — which means playing other people's entries is the price of competing. Files stay unlocked for the first 48 hours of rating for bug fixes and extra platform builds only. Hosted by Brackeys with moderators AquaXV, Yasahiro and Duxez, sponsored by web-games portal CrazyGames, with jam art based on a submission by community member Lara. Source: itch.io jam page · Entries

  • [ BUNDLE ] 324 Games for $10: itch.io's Games for Scholarships Charity Bundle Is Live Through September 20

    Organizer jesthehuman's back-to-school bundle collects everything submitted to the Games for Scholarships jam — 365 entries across video games, browser games and tabletop RPGs, made between July 13 and August 10 under a jam policy that barred discriminatory and AI-generated material. The resulting DRM-free package is 324 items from 159 creators for a $10 minimum, against a regular value of roughly $674. Every dollar goes to Games for Change, funding scholarships for underserved youth in the G4C Student Challenge, the nonprofit's global design competition for students aged 10 to 25. Collection opened August 17 and runs to September 20; as of this morning it has raised $8,556.11 of a $10,000 goal — 85% funded — from 706 contributors, who are averaging $12.11 each, comfortably above the asking price, with a new $75 top contribution. That is $939 and 74 more backers added in the past day, a shade faster than the day before, and at this rate it clears $10,000 inside the week with more than three weeks still left to run. Source: itch.io bundle page · Jam page

  • AUG 26 Folk Emerging — OUT NOW

    The first game from Curious Dynamics, a solo developer in the EU, is a turn-based 4X that stops where every other 4X starts. Rock Paper Shotgun called it "the most intriguing 4X in a long while, partly because it declines to entirely become one"; eXplorminate put it more bluntly — "the first 5 turns of Civilization, made into a game." You lead nomadic families through the Pleistocene, and the tech tree runs from controlled fire and cooking through domestication and agriculture to megalithic construction and the first cities, at which point you are done. Underneath is a simulation that is doing more than a 4X normally bothers with: nine climates each with a dynamic food web, procedural Earth-like maps generated from real climate simulation, finite resources whose extraction damages the environment, and many generations of individually simulated people with heritable traits, visualised as a social network so you can actually read the family trees and grudges. Culture evolves its own norms that change how neighbours trade and fight with you, combat resolves as an autobattler, and narrative events can bend a tribe's whole trajectory. The demo drew 84% positive across 26 reviews and pushed the launch from July so diplomacy, combat and AI could be reworked on Next Fest feedback. Windows via Steam, and the demo ran fine under Proton. Steam · GamingOnLinux · eXplorminate

  • AUG 26 Fatal Claw — 1.0, OUT OF EARLY ACCESS

    NDEV GAMES' hand-drawn metroidvania spent nine months in Early Access and came out the other side at 94% of 135 reviews. You play Kisha, a cat resurrected by his ancestors into an underground world where Ancients and Commoners live side by side among sealed passages, stones that archive vital force, and creatures turned violent by an infection. The central loop is absorption: beat the infected and take their abilities, then use them to reach the parts of the map that were closed to you. The zones are explicitly nonlinear and systematically interconnected, the pitch being that the order you find things in becomes your version of the story, and the ending is a choice rather than a reward — you pursue whatever you decided was worth pursuing. Roughly 10 hours for the main path. The 1.0 adds new areas and content on top of everything the Early Access build accumulated. Published by Com2uS Holdings with H2 Interactive. $24.99, cut 20% for launch. Windows via Steam. Steam · Netto's Game Room · PlayDay.One

  • AUG 25 Blood Dungeon — OUT NOW

    Messhof — Mark Essen and Kristy Norindr, the two people who made Nidhogg — are back with a platforming autoshooter, and they are self-publishing it again. You run, jump and climb through dungeon arenas while the shooting happens by itself; the whole game is about positioning. Ladders, grates, chains, ceilings and the water below are all traversal surfaces, and the 100-plus enemy types are built to punish lazy routes — some fly straight through walls, others stick to them or bounce off. Kills drop Blood, which raises your level mid-run and buys upgrades, while bones go to the Dogmancer for permanent unlocks between attempts. Nine bunkmates each rewrite the movement rather than just the weapon: Leggy spins climbable webs, Bug flies, Skinja is built for speed. Six survival arenas, revealed at Day of the Devs 2026, and the soundtrack gets billing in the store copy for good reason. Opening reviews are Positive — 40 of 41. $9.99, cut 15% to $8.49. Windows and macOS via Steam, plus PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Switch and Switch 2. Steam · Game Informer

  • AUG 25 Polylithic — 1.0, OUT OF EARLY ACCESS

    Czech studio polyperfect's Stone Age survival game left Early Access on its third anniversary and change, after more than 50 updates, with the biggest content drop it has ever shipped. The premise is that humans are not yet the apex predator and you are quite far down the list: lead a tribe from desperate Paleolithic foraging to the first permanent Neolithic settlements, crafting the earliest tools in human history and researching your way up. Its visual argument is against every other prehistoric game — this one is vibrantly coloured low-poly rather than the usual brown, across tall forests, snow-dusted hills and rapids that are indifferent to your survival. Hunting runs from rabbits to mammoths. The 1.0 adds companion animals, rival tribes, expanded customisation and a comedic nature-documentary framing over the whole thing, alongside online co-op for up to 10 and a procedural Sandbox Mode. Reviews sit Mixed at 69% of 209, carried over from the Early Access years. Published by indie.io. $14.99, cut 35% to $9.74. Windows via Steam and the indie.io store. Steam · indie.io · COGconnected

  • AUG 25 Hapunan — OUT NOW (itch.io and Steam)

    Yikon Games and Neuroticfly Games' PSX-styled psychological horror went from itch.io demo to full release on both stores the same day. Its subject is balut, the Filipino midnight street food, and the vendors who walk the graveyard shift calling "Baluuuut!" into empty streets. You play Niko, a teenager two months behind on rent and facing eviction by a ruthless landlord, who goes out selling with his father to keep the family's home — and finds the trade carries secrets that belong strictly to the dark, tangled up with the name Penduko and Philippine folklore. Choices dictate who eats and who doesn't. The developers have been iterating on this in public since a 2025 Android build; the itch page has run devlogs for well over a year, and today's carries the 1.0.5 Windows build alongside the Steam launch. $3.99, cut 10% to $3.59 on Steam; $3.99 or more on itch.io. Windows. Steam · itch.io · Devlog

  • AUG 25 ASTRAL COURT DX — FREE ON STEAM

    LUA COMPUTA's arcade precision platformer opens on death row in a prison in Heaven. The Astral Court, Heaven's only court and by its own description an odd one, offers The Prisoner bail on the condition of surviving a run of deadly trials — so you platform across the Scales of Justice themselves, several of them at once, collecting stars called Shinies while the arena fills with hazards. The movement is tuned for control over commitment: you can jump even after you've already started falling, then double-jump for height, which turns every near-miss into something recoverable. Multiple characters, and a chrome-and-cloud aesthetic that looks like a late-90s console demo disc. Free, and opening Very Positive at 97% of 67 reviews. Windows via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 24 Ultrapool — OUT NOW

    Icedrop Games' pool roguelike deckbuilder is exactly what the name promises: a billiards table wired to a shop economy. Pocketing balls scores points, points become money, and between rounds you spend it at the bar on new balls and upgrades — every ball carries its own effect, so a run turns into a hunt for the combination that chains. Ten escalating rounds per attempt, eight sets of 21 balls to complete, difficulty modifiers, and daily challenges with leaderboards. It's a Godot build localised into 10 languages and light enough for a dual-core with integrated graphics, which is how the same game reached iOS and Android the same day as Steam. Published by offbrand games, the label that put out Rivals of Aether II and teamed with Northernlion on Demon Bluff. A free demo is still up alongside the paid version. $11.99, cut 20% to $9.59. PC via Steam, plus iOS and Android. Steam · Google Play · Official site

  • AUG 23 Type Ride — Early Access

    NOT__OK's deckbuilding tower defense is played entirely through the keyboard: you type words to summon Ink Monsters from a custom deck, then push them down the lane to break the enemy base. The fiction earns the mechanic — a rogue state-of-the-art printer has flooded the world with ink lifeforms, and the protagonist, once the world's fastest typist and known as the "Type Rider," gets handed a typewriter-shaped summoning device and her old motorcycle. Between fights you draft from more than 50 cartridges; Overlay Cartridges stack on top of summoned monsters to grant abilities or raise stats, so builds compound the deeper a run goes, with items and conditional ultimates as escape valves. Steam achievements are in. $8.99, cut 10% to $8.09 for launch. Windows via Steam. Steam

  • AUG 23 GwenBlade Witcheresque — OUT NOW

    ArcWillow's soulslike puts its subject up front: Jane is stuck in a city that feeds on her doubts, grief and exhaustion, and the monsters she fights by night are born from her own shadow. The systems are built around that. You save at photobooths, heal at streetlamps and from a rechargeable lighter that only refills if you keep attacking, and when you die you drop everything and have to beat your Shadow — a copy that fights exactly the way you do — to get it back. Two currencies pull against each other: Happiness is spendable but fragile and easily lost, Misery is earned by taking risks and spent on growth, so the upgrade curve literally asks how much you're willing to suffer. Nightmare mode is the intended, punishing version; Daydream keeps the combat challenge but softens the systems around it. Combat is block, parry, kick and poise-break through an interconnected city with unlockable shortcuts. Opening reviews are Positive. $5.00, cut 40% to $3.00. Windows via Steam. Steam
