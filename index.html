  • [ TOOL ] Pixelorama v1.2 Lands — Keyframe Timeline, Autotiling, Drawing on 3D Models

    Orama Interactive shipped the biggest update yet to its free, open-source, Godot-built pixel-art editor. The headline is a new keyframe-based timeline that finally animates Pixelorama's non-destructive layer effects: keyframe the Offset & Scale effect on the first and last frame and a parallax background interpolates itself, and custom Godot shaders can be loaded as animatable effects too. Tilemap layers get autotiling through a new Tiles Property Tool, with terrain bits carried over when you export a Godot TileSet. The 3D layer system was rebuilt from scratch — objects are now shared across a whole layer, positions, meshes and material properties are all animatable, .gltf scenes import, and the pencil can draw straight onto 3D models (experimental). Also new: a Flat to Isometric effect, a theming system you can recolor from Preferences, and Steam Cloud project sync for Steam buyers. Mind the breaking changes — 3D layers and reference images saved in v1.1.x will not load: "make sure to finish them in v1.1.x and do not import them in v1.2." Source: itch.io devlog

  • [ DEVLOG ] Soulash 2 Ships v0.10.0 "The Living World" — Settlements That Run Themselves

    Artur Smiarowski (Wizards of the Code) posted the next major update to his god-versus-civilization roguelike this morning, alongside the game's first new trailer in three years. Settlements now have lives of their own whether or not you're watching — festivals in good times, rat infestations in bad, and lasting consequences if you leave a problem alone. The wilderness got the same treatment: animal migrations, roaming creatures, strange encounters, and NPC adventuring parties that raid, loot and grow in power on their own (you can track the active ones in the knowledge tab). Dynamic weather — storms, sandstorms, heatwaves — now sweeps across the map and rewrites the rules of exploration. Rounding it out: a new Impossible-tier savanna location, alchemy, carpentry and imbuing milestones, and minimap zoom buttons with gamepad support. Source: itch.io devlog

  • [ JAM ] LOWREZJAM 2026 Opens August 1 — 64×64 Pixels, Hard Ceiling

    itch.io's long-running resolution-constraint jam returns: build a game that renders at 64×64 pixels or less. Scaling the window up for display is fine, the game view is not — and hosts JackOatley and Gurpreet Singh Matharoo have zero patience for negotiation on that point: "Can I go higher than the 64x64 resolution? NO. That would just defy the point of the jam." Any engine, any language, solo or team, 2D or 3D, with ten optional unrated themes on offer. Submissions run August 1–16, community voting through August 23, and there is nothing to win — "Only the enjoyment of friendly competition within a great community." 212 jammers signed up so far. Source: itch.io

  • [ JAM ] B1T Jam 5 Starts July 31 — Two Colors, Ten Days, 513 Jammers

    The biggest jam kicking off this week on itch.io is also the strictest-looking: host Macrow's 1-bit jam allows "only any 2 colors (hex codes), modifying alpha or transparency is not allowed if it produces a new color." Two visible colors on screen, full stop. Generative AI is "prohibited, No exceptions." The theme drops when the clock starts July 31 at 22:00 UTC and runs to August 10, with awards for community vote, judges' pick, best-designed game page and best cover art — winners get a year of Craftpix and a front-page highlight. Source: itch.io
