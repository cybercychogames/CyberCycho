> ITCH.IO NEWS

  • [ JAM RESULTS ] GMTK Game Jam 2026 Becomes the Biggest Jam Ever Hosted on itch.io

    Mark Brown posted his favourites roundup on August 8, and the numbers underneath it are the real headline: more than 37,000 people signed up and over 10,500 games got submitted to this year's GMTK Game Jam, pushing it past Global Game Jam to become the largest jam itch.io has ever hosted. The theme, "Count Down," pushed entrants away from the usual power-fantasy escalation and toward numbers that shrink — 10,581 entries went up between July 22–26, and the community handed out 223,337 ratings across 10,407 of them before judging closed on July 31. Brown's picks lean toward the theme's sharper readings over the literal ones: Some Reassembly Required, his top pick, is a Zachtronics-style puzzle where you program a robot arm to build a spaceship with only ten instruction slots before liftoff; Picture Day has you contorting a face to match a target expression before the shutter's countdown hits zero; 11:59 is a frantic homework-submission sim racing actual midnight; and Count Your Clones turns single-player into a co-op puzzle by making every past run of yours repeat its actions alongside you. Submissions stay browsable on itch.io, and GMTK Game Jam 2027 is already dated for August 11. Source: Mark Brown, GMTK · Full results on itch.io

  • [ MILESTONE ] Dwarf Fortress Turns 20 — Kitfox Shows the First Art From Its Magic Update

    Patch 53.16 doubles as an anniversary card: twenty years after Tarn and Zach Adams put the first version online, Kitfox Games is marking it with a livestreamed show and the first public look at what comes after the current era of updates — Myth & Magic. New art from Thea Kent depicts "a dwarven wizard, a bunch of wild looking creatures and the God of Blood Armok himself," and composer Dabu paired with Simon Swewer on an accompanying track about "a dwarven wizard experimenting with the amazing, destructive powers of magic." The pair are taking the update to Gamescom at the end of the month for a first public gameplay showing, streamed for anyone not in Cologne. The anniversary show itself airs on Kitfox's Twitch channel with Tarn and Zach playing the original 2006 build next to the current one, plus the first-ever Beyond Quality Awards, honoring "the devs and community members who have contributed throughout the years." The patch underneath the celebration is mostly cleanup: unicorns and other fanciful creatures are back with properly tracked materials, tavern patrons are capped at one drink so taverns stop turning into medical emergencies, library visitors stop walking off with the books, and procedurally generated weapons and armor finally render with the materials they're actually made of. Source: itch.io devlog · Game page

  • [ DEVLOG ] Keepsake County 0.7.9 Adds a Haunted House and an Enemy You Cannot Tie Up

    Forward Instinct's midwestern burglary sim — rob a procedurally-generated farmhouse you've never seen before, in the minutes before the cops arrive — pushed its biggest beta update yet today. The headline is CONDEMNED, "a snowy, frozen haunted house to rob, loaded with backstory," and with it a heist-specific enemy that breaks the rules the rest of the game runs on: THE GHOST "makes no footstep noises, walks through doors, and cannot be tied or stunned." The house comes boarded up — sealed windows, doors, destroyed walls and rooftops — so the usual improvised entrances don't all work. Around it, the update finally gives the game somewhere to stand still: THE CAMP, a safe area built out of the trailer from the splash screen, holding a keepsake display of everything robbed in your current run ("this will be important to the story later..."). Two logs arrive alongside it — The Frikota Home Guide, recording every item you steal, what it was worth, whether it was heavy, and how many you actually extracted with, and The Scrapbook, which saves the notes and newspapers you grabbed mid-chase so you can read them somewhere safer. CRIMECITIES, reached through an in-fiction home VR system, is a consequence-free sandbox for testing tools, doors, hazards and the car, carefully walled off so nothing looted there counts and no unlockable leaks into the real game. Also in: chicken coops, flagpoles, grain silos and hedges, better window climbing, readables that slow time to 10% while you read, and Apricot Estates pulled for a redesign. The public beta is $14.99 on itch.io for Windows, macOS and Linux — four regular levels, three story levels and a tutorial across six biomes — and owners get every update through to the final game, Steam release included, for free. Source: itch.io devlog · Game page

  • [ HOMEBREW ] One Man, 130 Atari 2600 Games, and a Guinness Record 50 Cartridges Away

    MIRROR SHIP went up on itch.io today with a devlog title that doubles as a scoreboard: "MIRROR SHIP — 130th Atari 2600 Game." Marc Vittorini builds for the 1977 console and nothing else, and he is explicit about why: "I make games exclusively for the Atari 2600 and would like to get into the Guinness Book of Records as the single person/company to have made the most games for a single console. More than Atari itself." The target is at least 180 cartridges, and he wants them done by the end of this year. The funding model is as plain as the goal — the 40-plus games he put out last year stay free forever, while 2026's releases cost "the ridiculous price of $2." Number 130 is a two-ship puzzle-action game: a second vessel mirrors your every maneuver across the void, so each turn toward an energy orb is also a turn that might drive your reflection into a star. You hold one ship at a time, swapping with the fire button — the energy bar and score recolor to match whichever you're steering — and you must catch spheres of your own color; wrong color or a star costs energy, and the run ends when the energy is gone or the two ships crash into each other. The $2 download is a real cartridge package: ROM, PDF manual and a Stella bezel, with a free demo playable in the browser. The developer's one warning is emulator-specific — Javatari makes the image shake, Stella doesn't. Source: itch.io devlog · Game page

  • [ DEVLOG ] Baldi's Basics Plus 1.0 Is on Schedule — and Still Won't Say Who's Publishing It

    Basically Games posted its August status update on the Early Access edutainment-parody roguelike, and the news is mostly the absence of bad news: development of the 1.0 build is running to plan. Having finished the horizontal slice last time out — every important feature at a "placeholder sufficient" state — the work now is dragging each of those features to a finished one, which means "a lot of programming and game design work" against a schedule laid out with Nayan that runs to the end of this year, and which is explicitly not the whole schedule. Finalized audio and visual assets come after, and "that's also an area where a publisher should be able to help out quite a bit." On that front: still no announcement, but "there has been even more movement in this area since the last status update." Versions 0.14.3, 0.14.4 and 0.14.5 have shipped to Early Access in the meantime and 0.14 will keep getting occasional bugfix passes. So much of 1.0 is under wraps that the update's one screenshot has its spoilers painted over in red blocks — which the dev isn't worried about, since "so many of [your guesses] are so far from correct." Also announced: a Makeship plushie of Playtime, deliberately bald on the back of her head to match her scribbly MS-Paint sprite, already at 75% of its goal and only orderable until August 23. Source: itch.io devlog

  • [ DEVLOG ] Two Years Silent, NeuraQuarium Returns With the Update That Unblocks It

    urocyongames' neural-net evolution sim — critters with visible, editable brains swimming around eating each other — had painted itself into a corner. "Every new feature added more overhead, dragging performance," the developer writes, and years of accumulated tech debt were "ossifying the codebase" until experiments stopped taking and real-job work took over. v1.23 is the fix: brains, scent and heat dispersal, vision, taxonomy rendering and save/load have all been rewritten to behave identically but far faster, and the headroom went straight into scale — bigger maximum levels and a critter population cap raised from 750 to 1,500. The neural net renderer now draws curved synapses that animate their signal by type and fade everything but the hovered neuron's connections; a visual-field overlay shows a selected critter's vision range and current foveal focus. The taxonomy tree got elbow-style ancestry lines, filters for relatives of living species, a zoomed-in stat breakdown and an AutoTag feature that names significant branches for you. Old saves still load. And after years of the tiny green dots being called "fruit," they are now, correctly, ALGAE. $4.95, Windows, macOS and Linux. Source: itch.io devlog

  • [ BUNDLE ] Game Industry Hardship Fund Bundle Closes In On Its $200K Goal, Four Days Left

    Necrosoft Games (Demonschool) is hosting an itch.io bundle with 109 other creators to bankroll the United Videogame Workers–CWA Local 9433's hardship fund, which pays cash to laid-off and under-employed game developers across the US and Canada — two application tiers, under $1,000 and $1,000–$5,000, with roughly $25,000 already distributed to 30 applicants out of union dues before the bundle existed. 127 DRM-free items normally worth about $1,023 go for $10: A Short Hike, SkateBIRD, Cook, Serve, Delicious!, They Bleed Pixels, Wheels of Aurelia, Lucah: Born of a Dream, NEON STRUCT and more. Live since July 30 and running through August 13, it has now pulled $185,914.21 from 13,451 contributors — 92% of a goal the organizers reset to $200,000 after blowing past their original $100,000 target in four days. The organizers' framing is blunt: many applicants "have lost housing, are on food assistance, or have put off necessary medical treatment." Source: itch.io · Aftermath

  • [ JAM ] LOWREZJAM 2026 Now Open — 64×64 Pixels, Hard Ceiling

    itch.io's long-running resolution-constraint jam is under way: build a game that renders at 64×64 pixels or less. Scaling the window up for display is fine, the game view is not — and hosts JackOatley and Gurpreet Singh Matharoo have zero patience for negotiation on that point: "Can I go higher than the 64x64 resolution? NO. That would just defy the point of the jam." Any engine, any language, solo or team, 2D or 3D, with ten optional unrated themes on offer. Submissions run August 1–16, community voting through August 23, and there is nothing to win — "Only the enjoyment of friendly competition within a great community." 330 jammers have signed up, with the first 4 entries already in. Source: itch.io

  • [ JAM ] B1T Jam 5 Running Through August 10 — Two Colors, 756 Jammers

    The biggest jam to kick off at the end of July on itch.io is also the strictest-looking: host Macrow's 1-bit jam allows "only any 2 colors (hex codes), modifying alpha or transparency is not allowed if it produces a new color." Two visible colors on screen, full stop. Generative AI is "prohibited, No exceptions." The theme drops when the clock started July 31 at 22:00 UTC and runs to August 10, with awards for community vote, judges' pick, best-designed game page and best cover art — winners get a year of Craftpix and a front-page highlight. Source: itch.io

  • [ CHARITY BUNDLE ] Games for Scholarships Jam Building a Bundle to Fund Youth Game Designers

    Host jesthehuman is collecting submissions through August 9 for a charity bundle benefiting Games for Change — proceeds fund scholarships that let underserved youth aged 10–25 enter the G4C Student Challenge, the nonprofit's global student game-design competition. Contributors approve their entries by August 16, and the bundle itself runs August 17 – September 20 at a $10 minimum. 175 creators have joined, contributing 322 entries to the pool so far. Source: itch.io

> INDIE GAME RELEASES — JULY–AUGUST 2026

  • AUG 7 Chop Chop Inc. — OUT NOW

    Darmstadt studio rokaplay — former Endzone developers making "cozy and family-friendly games" — launched a first-person job sim built on a running joke: after Furnox Corp lays you off, you turn a one-axe workshop into a furniture empire out of spite. Chop trees (including the unusual metal, stone and tech varieties), process wood into planks, automate the production lines and sell increasingly impractical creations — a heat-conducting wooden PC, a wooden weight bench — for bonus cash while befriending the eccentric cast along the way. Over 150,000 wishlists going in. €12.99, PC via Steam. Steam · Saving Content

  • AUG 7 Expeditions: Samurai — Early Access

    Logic Artists and THQ Nordic put the fourth Expeditions game into Early Access the same day as THQ Nordic's digital showcase. You land in Sengoku-era Japan aboard a battered Dutch ship as a privateer — not William Adams — commanding a party through a civil war where stealth, diplomacy and violence are all treated as full alternatives to each other rather than a combat game with dialogue bolted on. Reception out of the gate is genuinely mixed: 56% positive from around 120 reviews, with players flagging bugged death animations, missing voice acting and lines that overlap when skipped, but several previews describe a deep, ambitious RPG that a few more patches could turn into "a new instant classic." Full release is targeted for Q1 2027. PC via Steam, discounted 34% through August 14. Steam · PC Gamer

  • AUG 6 Grim Axiom — OUT NOW

    Rare Soup's isometric souls-like adventure-horror game turns the camera angle into a resource fight: you wake underground with amnesia, in a labyrinth of mutated monsters lit only by the lantern you're carrying, and the light itself becomes something to manage alongside stamina and spacing. Healing only happens at checkpoints, so every fight either costs health you can't easily get back or ends with you sneaking past instead. Weapons and armor carry distinct stats to build around, and herbs scavenged along the way brew into temporary buffs. 83% positive out of the gate. PC via Steam, 10% launch discount through August 16. Steam · GameGrin

  • AUG 6 IRON NEST: Heavy Turret Simulator — OUT NOW

    A two-person team — Nick Nieuwoudt and Dominik Latos, 20-plus years of combined experience — shipped a dieselpunk heavy-artillery sim set in an alternate-history Spain of the late 1920s. You crew a 5,000-ton war machine from the inside: elevation wheels, levers and ballistic calculators are all hands-operated rather than menu-driven, aimed at 15 playable regions of procedurally generated objectives across a story told partly through newspaper reports tracking the consequences of what you just did. Over 30 unlockable ammunition types serve different tactical purposes, and there's a customizable cat companion riding along for the "military-grade hearing loss" of it all. Already sitting at 99% positive from over 1,000 reviews; Kotaku called it "tactile and absurdly meticulous, but god does it feel satisfying." Steam · Release date confirmation

  • AUG 6 ReStory: Chill Electronics Repairs — OUT NOW

    Mandragora and publisher tinyBuild's narrative shop-management sim puts you behind the counter of an electronics repair shop in mid-2000s Tokyo, cleaning, part-swapping and reassembling Y2K-era devices — cell phones, music players, and officially licensed Atari consoles among them — sourced through an in-fiction retro web interface. Customer interactions branch into multiple endings depending on how you handle them, on top of the straightforward loop of managing shop finances and orders. Shacknews called it "a cozy game for retro gaming nerds"; it's landed at 99% positive from 200-plus reviews. Steam · Shacknews preview

  • AUG 6 Doloc Town — 1.0 OUT NOW

    RedSaw Games Studio's side-scrolling post-apocalyptic farming sim closes out Early Access after roughly 15 months, published by Logoi Games and Pathea Games (My Time at Sandrock). The pitch is farming built vertically up a wasteland cliffside instead of sprawling across flat fields, layered with fishing, ranching, genetic crop modification and a new farm-automation system. Version 1.0 adds the Old City Ruins area — new enemies, NPCs, side quests and resources — and finally delivers the ending to the main story that Early Access left hanging. 95% positive from nearly 2,000 reviews. Steam · Gematsu

  • AUG 5 Kill The Shadow — OUT NOW

    A year ago this game wasn't on Steam at all. Shanghai studio Shadowlight started it as a student project in 2022, five recent graduates collecting awards at Chinese dev competitions until Hypergryph — the Arknights studio — invested in 2023 and pulled them into its COREBLAZER incubator. Then the deal with publisher 663 Games collapsed, the game was delisted, and by the producer known as Black's account the team was unpaid and close to disbanding. The listing came back in March 2025, NEOWIZ signed a global publishing deal that June, and today it ships with Phoenix Game credited as publisher. What survived is a cyber-noir detective RPG of 2D pixel characters over 3D environments, set in Dark Tide City, a metropolis split by a decade of civil war. You play Lucas, an ex-detective with a supernatural entity fastened to his back that can reconstruct the final moments of the dead — it cannot change the past, only replay it, so the work is watching the scene as it actually happened and catching where a witness's story contradicts it. Clues go on a corkboard, and the game doesn't funnel you to one answer; Charisma, Reasoning and Strength open different routes through a case rather than different difficulties. It starts with a police captain's dead dog and ends up somewhere considerably worse. The demo sits at 93% positive from 126 reviews. Steam · Tech Times · RPGamer

  • AUG 5 Fields of Mistria — 1.0 OUT NOW

    Live since noon ET today — two years to the day after it hit Early Access, NPC Studio's pixel farming RPG got its finished form. The Chicago studio — co-directed by Claire Belton and Andrew Duff, who created Pusheen before this — put out a game that cleared 100,000 downloads in its first week and 250,000 in its first month on the strength of an art style and soundtrack that had people arguing it out-farmed Stardew Valley. Version 1.0 finally closes the loops it shipped with: relationship caps rise to 10 hearts, bringing proposals, wedding events, marriage and children; the town-repair storyline becomes completable; Renown goes to level 100; and there are new dungeon quests and rewards, skill perks, furniture, cosmetics, mount skins and achievements. Beta localizations land in Simplified and Traditional Chinese, French, Japanese, Korean, Russian and Spanish. The promise is that "the game and storylines will be completable as originally envisioned." PC only at $13.99 — consoles are a maybe, and only after launch. Steam · NPC Studio announcement

  • AUG 4 Big Walk — OUT NOW

    Six years after Untitled Goose Game, House House's follow-up went live yesterday at 9am ET / 1pm UTC, published by Panic. It's a cooperative "walker-talker": a wide-open bushland world you cross with 2 to 12 friends — "i.e. a focused co-op experience" at the low end, "i.e. an absolute mess" at the high end — where the real puzzle is staying in contact. Proximity voice chat fades voices over distance, echoes them down corridors, muffles them through walls and crackles them through walkie-talkies; when talking isn't an option you're left pointing, waving and flailing. The toybox runs from laser pointers, binoculars and megaphones to flares, whiteboards, cowbells and "a big golden head (???)". Crossplay is in, and unlike most co-op sandboxes it's hand-crafted with an actual ending. The Melbourne studio isn't relaxed about it: designer Nico Disseldorp says he's "terrified" — "just the fact that it's been so long means you want the investment to have been worth it" — while Stuart Gillespie-Cook explains the overrun simply, "Big Walk kind of had a scope of its own." PC (Steam, Epic), PS5 and Switch 2, plus day-one on PS Plus Essential through August 31. Steam · GoNintendo interview · PlayStation LifeStyle

  • AUG 3 Crown Of Silence — Early Access

    Nyro Interactive's self-published 2D Metroidvania went into Early Access on August 3, and it wears its Souls-like influences openly: colossal bosses with fixed attack patterns, an emphasis on patience over reflexes, and a story told through crumbled statues and background detail rather than cutscenes — "every ruin whispers a fragment of a past tragedy." Rather than locking you into a class, it hands you weapons, armour and magical artifacts and expects you to re-equip per enemy, swapping between breaking guards with heavy strikes and dodging out of reach. The labyrinth underneath is classic genre construction: a silenced gothic kingdom that opens up as you find the tools to get deeper into it. Steam · GameGrin

  • AUG 3 Kin and Conquest — OUT NOW

    The Fox Knocks shipped 1.0 of its incremental action-adventure simultaneously on itch.io and Steam, ending a run of public 0.5.x builds — "Kin and Conquest is officially released," reads the entire devlog. The Evil Geomancer has taken the mountain and conjured fiends from across the land to hold it; the sacred Evertree picks you to go in. You guide a fox with nothing but the mouse cursor — keyboard and gamepad optional — and combat resolves by proximity, your fox swinging automatically whenever something gets close, so the play is positioning and dodging rather than clicking attacks. Progression stacks equipment, upgrades, a skill tree and permanent Blessings on top of each other. $8.99 on Steam with a 10% launch discount (co-published with Gamersky Games), or $9.99 name-your-own-price on itch.io; ten languages at launch. Steam · itch.io devlog

  • JUL 31 Sephiria — 1.0 OUT NOW

    TEAM HORAY, the Yongin studio behind Dungreed, took its action roguelite out of Early Access on July 31 after roughly a year and a 97% positive review record. Bunnyville's Day of Destiny arrives, demons pour out of the tower next door, and one of the townsfolk — cute animals, absurd weapons — goes in to end it. The 1.0 build adds the sixth and final chapter plus new weapons, items and talents, closing out a campaign with six weapon types carrying 50+ upgrades each, 60-plus enemy types and 10-plus bosses, and an inventory built around Artifacts and Tablets. Up to four players can run it in online co-op, trading items and reviving each other. PC and Mac via Steam, $8.99 in the launch sale. Steam · Saving Content

  • JUL 31 The Relic: First Guardian — OUT NOW

    Six years of work by a core team of about a dozen at Project Cloud Games, out digitally via Perp Games on PC and PS5 (Xbox Series and Switch 2 later this year, physical PS5 on September 4). You're the last guardian of the apocalyptic realm of Arsilthus, recovering lost memories — Relics — to restore a broken world. The souls-like twist is that stamina is reserved strictly for defence and dodging, so attacking costs you nothing; traditional level-ups are gone too, replaced by 70+ collectible Relics with distinct passive effects to build around. The launch has been bumpy on the technical side: the studio shipped two patches on day one and a third the same evening, saying its "current focus [is] fully on fixing technical issues and performance." A Night of the Unextinguished Flame DLC pack is out alongside it. Steam · Perp Games dev update

  • JUL 31 Corsair Cove — OUT NOW

    Limbic Entertainment (Tropico 6, Park Beyond) and Hooded Horse turn the city builder sideways — or rather upright. Your pirate haven doesn't sprawl outward across a deserted island, it climbs: buildings cling to cliff faces, stitched together by bridges, ropes and walkways until the whole thing grows into a vertical town, with production chains covering 50+ goods feeding the harbor and fleet below. Four progression paths — Notoriety, Empire, Seafaring and Wealth — unlock their own missions, buildings and ships. GameStar's Maurice Weber, four hours into the demo, called it "one of the most original city-builders in a long time," with long-term depth the open question. PC only: Steam, Epic and the Microsoft Store, day one on PC Game Pass. Steam · COGconnected · Notebookcheck
